\begin{abstract}
Editions of Old English mark long vowels with macrons, while the manuscripts mark length only sporadically, so the
bare text merges \emph{god} `God' and \emph{gōd} `good'. We restore the marks by choosing, for each bare word, among
the length patterns that candidate sources list, and we ask what limits the result. We build a length-marked corpus of
nine editions printed from 1880 to 1921 (\NCorpusTokens{} tokens) and release it with Tauber's \textit{Beowulf}, to
our knowledge the first such running text beyond that poem. Coverage limits verse most: \NPUnseenBeo\% of the
\textit{Beowulf} tokens never occur in the mostly prose training editions, dictionaries raise a frequency prior from
\NTBeoAllFreq\% to \NABeoAllFreq\%, and with WikiPron candidates \NPOutOfReachWBeoShare\% of the prior's verse errors
have no correct candidate. Context helps where grammar decides. On prose, a ByT5 scorer that sees two words on each
side raises five closed-class words, chiefly \emph{þe/þē}, from \NEncCapfullSolClsBWord\% to
\NEncCapfullSolClsBLocalTwo\% and leaves lexical and editorial ambiguity unchanged, and a post hoc logistic model with
word-specific collocations does about as well. On all ambiguous verse tokens one unseen name cancels the gain.
Convention moves the score itself. Wyatt's and Klaeber's \textit{Beowulf} agree on \NPairWTLex\% of ambiguous tokens
but on only \NPairWTClsB\% of these grammatical words, because Wyatt's text leaves \emph{nē}, \emph{gȳt} and some
relative \emph{þe} unmarked, and swapping the gold edition moves the prior's score on them by \NPairWTClsBPriorGap{}
points, \NPSwapRatio{} times the gain from context on the same tokens. CharsiuG2P's published Old English word error
rate (\NCharsiuWer) partly reflects a 20-step decoding limit (\NCharsiuWerFixed{} without it). An LLM labelled the
ambiguity classes and error types, and one of us checked every label.

\medskip\noindent Code, data and results: \url{https://github.com/Ace3Z/coverage-context-convention}.\\
Dataset: \url{https://huggingface.co/datasets/Ace3Z/old-english-vowel-length}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Modern editions of Old English mark long vowels with a macron, while the manuscripts mark length only sporadically
\cite{mokrowiecki2015}. Bare text therefore merges \emph{god} `God' and \emph{gōd} `good', or the relative particle
\emph{þe} and the pronoun \emph{þē} `thee'. Restoring the marks means choosing, for each bare word, among the length
patterns that candidate sources list. This is lexical disambiguation as candidate selection, as in accent and
diacritic restoration \cite{yarowsky1994,esmail2022,gershuni2022} and homograph disambiguation for grapheme-to-phoneme
(G2P) conversion \cite{gorman2018}, here for a historical language with little data. The ambiguity sits at three
linguistic levels, the lexicon (class~A below), syntax (class~B) and editorial habit (class~C). We predict an editor's
marking convention and make no claim about pronunciation.

We measure three limits on the same test tokens. \emph{Coverage} asks whether the right candidate exists: with
WikiPron candidates, \NPOutOfReachWBeoShare\% of a frequency prior's errors on \textit{Beowulf} have none.
\emph{Context} asks whether neighbouring words can choose: two words on each side raise prose class~B from
\NEncCapfullSolClsBWord\% to \NEncCapfullSolClsBLocalTwo\% and barely move the other classes. \emph{Convention} asks
whose marking the gold follows: swapping the gold between two \textit{Beowulf} editions moves the prior's class~B
score by \NPairWTClsBPriorGap{} points, \NPSwapRatio{} times the context gain on the same tokens. We contribute a
length-marked corpus of nine editions (to our knowledge the first beyond \textit{Beowulf}), an evaluation that
separates the three limits, a reanalysis of CharsiuG2P \cite{zhu2022}, and code that regenerates every number here.

\paragraph{Related work.} Yarowsky \cite{yarowsky1994} restored Spanish and French accents with decision lists over
word-specific collocations. Latin \cite{winge2015} and Ancient Greek \cite{cleland2026} macronizers use lemmatisers
and morphological analysers or large pretraining \cite{cullhed2026}, while we start from bare text. Gershuni and
Pinter \cite{gershuni2022} restore Hebrew diacritics without a dictionary, the generative alternative to candidate
selection, and Esmail et al.\ \cite{esmail2022} measure how much lookahead helps Arabic diacritisation, as our left
and sentence arms do.  To our knowledge, Tauber's \textit{Beowulf} \cite{tauber2021} is the only earlier length-marked
running text and no system has restored Old English vowel length before. Plank \cite{plank2022} argues that one gold
label hides annotator variation, and editorial convention is such variation.

\section{Data set}\label{sec:data}
Table~\ref{tab:data} lists the corpus: nine student editions printed from 1880 to 1921 with \NCorpusTokens{} tokens in
\NPCorpusSentences{} sentences, plus Tauber's \textit{Beowulf}. We took Project Gutenberg and Wikisource
transcriptions \cite{editions}, removed English and Latin lines and cleaned them with mappings counted in a manifest.
Some files come from lost extraction scripts and are released as committed. The Hugging Face release (CC BY-SA 4.0)
keeps each sentence's work, editor, year, genre and section, with per-token length patterns and a leakage flag. To our
knowledge it is the only openly licensed length-marked running text besides \textit{Beowulf}, which it exceeds
\NPCorpusOverTauber{} times. Word lists lack context, and treebanks such as YCOE \cite{ycoe2003} carry, as far as we
know, no length marks.

Our Harrison and Sharp file is a Project Gutenberg revision that modernised some quantities, such as conjunctive
\emph{ne} to \emph{nē}.

\section{Preprocessing}\label{sec:prep}
Preprocessing normalises Unicode (NFC), keeps the macron as the only diacritic, drops English and Latin lines by their
function words, splits sentences at end punctuation and takes letter runs as tokens, rejecting any token whose
unmarked form differs from the bare input. A token's \emph{key} is its lower-cased form without macrons, and its
\emph{pattern} has one digit per vowel letter, 1 for a macron: Harrison and Sharp's \emph{geārdagum} (0100) and
Klaeber's \emph{gēardagum} (1000) share the key \emph{geardagum}, which also shows a placement convention.

A key's candidates are its training patterns plus those of every listed form with that key. Our main list is WikiPron
\cite{lee2020}, whose IPA we convert to spellings by aligning vowel units with IPA nuclei (\NWikiAligned\% of
\NWikiRows{} rows align). A coverage comparison adds the dictionaries of our proposal, Clark Hall \cite{clarkhall},
Wiktionary via kaikki.org \cite{ylonen2022} and Bosworth-Toller \cite{bosworth}, though WikiPron is itself mined from
Wiktionary (\NPWikiInWiktionaryShare\% of its forms are in the Wiktionary list). A token is \emph{lexicon-ambiguous}
when its key has two or more candidates, as for \NPLexShareSol\% of prose and \NPLexShareBeo\% of verse tokens with
WikiPron. This set grows with the source, so sources are compared on all tokens. The \NPClassLabels{} most frequent
ambiguous keys (ranked with test tokens counted, not their gold) carry a class: \NClassA{} lexical (A,
\emph{god/gōd}), \NClassB{} grammatical (B: \emph{þe, ne, ge, gyt, þis}) and \NClassC{} editorial (C, \emph{se/sē,
he/hē}), covering \NPClsShareSol\% of prose and \NPClsShareBeo\% of verse ambiguous tokens.

\paragraph{Labels.} The classes, the types of \NPErrLabels{} sampled errors and a sample of \NCheckN{} editor
disagreements were labelled by a large language model, Claude Opus 5.5 \cite{claude2026}, run as separate annotator
agents with written guidelines. For the classes and the error types, two runs of the model labelled every item
separately (Cohen's $\kappa$ \NClassKappa{} and \NErrKappa{} \cite{cohen1960}), and a third run settled their
disagreements. A single run labelled the disagreement sample. A blind review by further runs of the same model agreed
with \NPReviewAgreed{} of the \NPReviewItems{} labels and changed \NPReviewChanged. One of us (Mahbod Tajdini) then
checked all \NPReviewItems{} labels by hand against the guidelines and changed none. As both annotators are runs of
one model, $\kappa$ measures that model's consistency rather than agreement between people, and with no second human
annotator we cannot report the human agreement that LLM labels need \cite{pangakis2023}.

\section{Model training}\label{sec:training}
\paragraph{Baselines.} All systems are supervised, choose among the same candidates and output all short for a word
without any. The frequency prior, fixed in our proposal after Yarowsky \cite{yarowsky1994}, picks a word's most
frequent training pattern and, for an unseen word, the pattern most sources list, with ties going to fewer long
vowels. Logistic classifiers (scikit-learn \cite{pedregosa2011}, untuned, three seeds) see sparse indicators of the
word and its first and last two letters (\emph{word}), plus the two preceding words (\emph{left}), plus the two
following words (\emph{bilateral}). These context features are not conjoined with the word, so `previous word
\emph{tō}' shifts every word's scores alike. After the ByT5 results we added a post hoc \emph{conjoined} variant with
Yarowsky-style word-by-neighbour features.

\paragraph{ByT5.} Our main model fine-tunes ByT5-small \cite{xue2022} with Transformers \cite{wolf2020} to score
candidates. The input is the bare word, alone or in context with the target marked \texttt{[[ ]]}, the output is its
pattern string, and a candidate's score is the summed log-probability of its bytes, free of length bias since
candidates are equally long. Arms differ only in the input: \emph{word}, \emph{left} (all preceding words),
\emph{sentence}, \emph{local2} and \emph{local5} (two or five words on each side), all trained on the same
\NEncTrainExamples{} ambiguous training tokens as the logistic models. ByT5 reads bytes, so macrons, thorn and eth
need no vocabulary, it is CharsiuG2P's backbone, and scoring candidates guarantees a valid output. We report the full
input cap (\NEncFullCap{} bytes), as caps of 128 and 256 showed no effect.

\paragraph{Protocol.} We chose the setting (learning rate $3\cdot10^{-4}$, \NSelEpochs{} epochs, no Zupitza) from
\NSelRuns{} sentence-arm runs on development data and reused it for every arm. Dropping Zupitza's \textit{Grammar},
which marks \emph{þes}, \emph{gyt} and \emph{lytel} short, lifts the prior on ambiguous development tokens from
\NDevFreqZupShared\% to \NDevFreqNoZup\% and the bilateral model from \NDevBilZupShared\% to \NDevBilNoZup\%. ByT5 is
consistent with this without supporting it: its best setting improves from \NSelDevZupShared\% to \NPSelDevExact\%,
but over the \NPDThreeEncSettings{} matched settings without a collapsed run the change spans $\NPDThreeEncMin$ to
$\NPDThreeEncMax$ points. The price, measured afterwards, is coverage: only Zupitza writes \emph{forþām},
\emph{swāswā} and \emph{forþī} as one word, so \NPZupWords{} prose test tokens become unknown and the prior loses
\NDThreeSolAllCost{} points. The grid has \NGridRuns{} runs (four context arms at three caps and the word arm, three
seeds each). About fifty exploratory runs were scored on the test sets before the development set existed and
selection runs logged test scores, but the choice used development scores only.

\paragraph{Changes from the proposal.} The corpus grew to nine editions. A ByT5 encoder-decoder, trained on a lab GPU
server instead of Colab, replaced the planned encoder classifier. WikiPron became the main candidate source, with the
proposed dictionaries in a coverage comparison. An LLM, checked by one of us, labelled what we had planned to label
ourselves, and we set the threshold for poor editor agreement only after measuring, so it decided nothing.

\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}
We score exact whole-pattern token accuracy: a token counts only if every vowel has the gold length. Marking nothing
scores \NPAllShortSol\% (prose) and \NPAllShortBeo\% (verse). Differences carry 95\% intervals from a paired bootstrap
over sentences \cite{efron1993} (1,000 draws, correctness averaged over runs), conditional on the trained runs, and
headline ByT5 contrasts also resample runs. Between ByT5 arms we claim nothing below about one point.

\subsection{Coverage}\label{sec:coverage}
Figure~\ref{fig:coverage} sets the prior against the achievable ceiling, above which no chooser can help. Dictionaries
change the prior only for words unseen in training, since a dictionary-only pattern has a training count of zero: on
unseen verse tokens the prior rises from \NPUnseenPriorTBeo\% to \NPUnseenPriorABeo\%. With WikiPron,
\NPOutOfReachWSolShare\% of its prose errors and \NPOutOfReachWBeoShare\% of its verse errors have no correct
candidate, mostly because the word is unknown, although our proposal assumed that every error is a selection error. A
better chooser could gain at most \NPHeadroomSolPts{} points on prose and \NPHeadroomBeoPts{} on verse. The verse
dictionary gain is specific to \textit{Beowulf}: \NPBeoOnlyGainShare\% of it falls on word types found in no other
text of the corpus.

\subsection{Context}\label{sec:context}
On prose, local2 beats the word arm by \dci{NEncCapfullSolLexLocalTwoVsWord} points on ambiguous tokens
(Table~\ref{tab:context}), and class~B carries all of it: \NPTeSolClsB{} token-equivalents (correct tokens averaged
over runs) against \NPTeSolLex{} in total. There it lifts accuracy from \NEncCapfullSolClsBWord\%, where the word arm
equals the prior, to \NEncCapfullSolClsBLocalTwo\% (\dci{NEncCapfullSolClsBLocalTwoVsWord}, or
\iv{\NPTwoLevelSolClsBLocalTwoVsWordLo}{\NPTwoLevelSolClsBLocalTwoVsWordHi} when runs are resampled too). Every
context arm shows this gain, and classes A and C barely move. Most of it is relative \emph{þe} against the pronoun
\emph{þē} `thee' (\NPPwSolThePrior\% to \NPPwSolTheLocalTwo\% on \NPPwSolTheN{} tokens). In \emph{Ic clypie tō þē,
Drihten, þū þe æall geworhtest} `I call to thee, Lord, thou who madest all', the prior and every logistic and ByT5
word run mark both \emph{þe} short, and every local2 run gets the pronoun long and the relative short. Of the
\NToTheN{} long \emph{þē} after \emph{tō} in the text, word runs find \NToTheEncWordMax{} and local2 runs
\NToTheEncLocalTwoMin{} to \NToTheEncLocalTwoMax.

On verse class~B, local2 gains \dci{NEncCapfullBeoClsBLocalTwoVsWord} points, almost all from \emph{þe}, yet on all
ambiguous verse tokens no context arm beats the word arm (local2 \dci{NEncCapfullBeoLexLocalTwoVsWord}) because of one
name. \emph{Gēata} `of the Geats' (\NPGeataN{} tokens, all long) is unseen in training, and WikiPron also lists
\emph{geata} `gates'. The prior falls back to fewer long vowels and misses every token, the word arm gets all of them
in every seed, and the sentence arm none. Without \emph{Gēata}, local2 beats the word arm by
\dci{NPBeoLexNoGeataLocalTwoVsWord} points (a post hoc analysis). On all tokens local2 adds \NPEncGainLocalTwoSol{}
(prose) and \NPEncGainLocalTwoBeo{} points (verse) to the WikiPron prior (the planned sentence arm \NPEncGainBilSol{}
and \NPEncGainBilBeo), of the \NPHeadroomSolPts{} and \NPHeadroomBeoPts{} a better chooser could add, while
dictionaries add \NSolGainDictWiki{} and \NBeoGainDictWiki.

The bilateral logistic model gains less over its word model (prose class~B \dci{NBootSolClsBBilVsWord}), as its
features cannot express that `previous word \emph{tō}' matters for \emph{þe} alone. The post hoc conjoined variant
can, and on prose it is level with local2 (local2 minus conjoined: \dci{NPLocalTwoVsConjSolLex} on ambiguous tokens,
\dci{NPLocalTwoVsConjSolClsB} on class~B), so on our prose a linear model with word-specific collocations matches
ByT5. Training variance limits the table: per run, prose class~B spans \NEncRunsSolClsBBilMin\% to
\NEncRunsSolClsBBilMax\% for the sentence arm. Local inputs never reach a cap, so local runs of one seed repeat the
same training at each cap, yet only local2 replicates match bit for bit (\NPIdenticalLocalTwoRuns{} of
\NPIdenticalLocalTwoOf{} runs), and local5 replicates differ by up to \NEncRepSolClsBLocalFiveSpread{} points on prose
class~B. We set no deterministic mode and did not test the cause \cite{dodge2020,summers2021}, and three runs per arm
cannot rank the context arms.

\subsection{Convention}\label{sec:convention}
Table~\ref{tab:editors} compares the three \textit{Beowulf} editions on the tokens each pair aligns (identical
spelling runs, \NPAlignMin\% to \NPAlignMax\% of each text). The revised Harrison and Sharp file sits close to
Klaeber, so Wyatt against Klaeber is our two-editor reference. They agree on \NPairWTLex\% of ambiguous tokens but
\NPairWTClsB\% of class~B, and in \NPWTClsBDisWyattShort{} of their \NPWTClsBDis{} class~B disagreements Wyatt's text
has the short form: both mark the pronouns \emph{þē} `thee' and \emph{gē} `ye' long, but only Klaeber marks \emph{nē}
`nor', \emph{gȳt} `yet', \emph{gē} `and' and some relative \emph{þe}. Of a labelled sample of \NCheckN{} Harrison and
Sharp and Wyatt disagreements, \NCheckEditorial{} are editorial and \NCheckOther{} other, mostly apparent slips, so
agreement between the transcriptions is a lower bound for the printed editions. On the \NPairWTClsBN{} class~B tokens
that Wyatt and Klaeber share, the prior agrees with Wyatt on \NPairWTClsBPriorWyatt\% and with Klaeber on
\NPairWTClsBPriorTauber\%, a swing of \NPairWTClsBPriorGap{} points, and against Wyatt it exceeds the editors' own
agreement, so agreement between editions is no ceiling on accuracy. Local2 gains \dci{NPSwapGainTauber} points over
the word arm with Klaeber as gold and \dci{NPSwapGainWyatt} with Wyatt, so the swing is \NPSwapRatio{} times the
context gain.

The article \emph{se} shows convention most clearly. Klaeber writes it short in \NPSeArticleShort{} of \NPSeArticle{}
article tokens and long in all \NPSeOtherLong{} pronoun and relative tokens, while our training editions write
\emph{sē} \NPSeTrainLong{} times against \NPSeTrainShort{} \emph{se} (only Sweet's \textit{Primer} and the
\textit{Physiologus} prefer \emph{se}). The prior gets \NPSeArticlePrior{} of the short articles right and no ByT5 run
more than \NPSeArticleBestRun, and in line 2587, \emph{þæt se mǣra maga Ecgðēowes}, the prior and all
\NPSeLineLongRuns{} runs mark it long. Klaeber's choice follows syntax and could be learned from Klaeber-style text,
but a model cannot tell from bare text which convention the gold follows.

\subsection{Errors and CharsiuG2P}\label{sec:errors}
Table~\ref{tab:errors} types \NErrItems{} random ambiguous errors from each of the ByT5 sentence arm (seed~1) and
CharsiuG2P. ByT5's prose errors are most often grammatical (\NPErrEncSolGrammatical{} of \NPErrEncSolN, \emph{þe}
\NPErrEncSolGramThe{} times) and its verse errors most often homographs (\NPErrEncBeoHomograph{} of \NPErrEncBeoN,
\emph{Gēata} \NPErrEncBeoHomGeata{} times). The two systems' profiles differ within verse ($\chi^2$, $p =
\NPErrChiBeo$) but not pooled over both tests (Fisher's exact tests, $p = \NPErrFisherAllHomograph$,
\NPErrFisherAllGrammatical{} and \NPErrFisherAllEditorial{} for A, B and C), as the genres pull apart. A fixed rule
decides \NPErrRuleDecided{} of the \NPErrLabels{} items, so label agreement is high partly by construction ($\kappa$
\NPErrKappaNoClass{} on the \NPErrItemsNoClass{} items without a class). In \textit{Beowulf} line 2796, \emph{ēcum
dryhtne þē ic hēr on starie}, Klaeber's and Harrison and Sharp's texts mark the relative \emph{þē} long and Wyatt's
does not. Every full-cap ByT5 run writes \emph{þe}, agreeing with one edition and scored against another.

\paragraph{CharsiuG2P.} CharsiuG2P \cite{zhu2022} is a multilingual ByT5 model trained on WikiPron word lists that
reads isolated words. We run it as published (prefix \texttt{<ang>:}), as an off-the-shelf reference, and map its IPA
to patterns with our aligner, counting unmappable outputs as wrong. We reproduce its published Old English scores
exactly (WER \NCharsiuWer, PER \NCharsiuPer). Its evaluation stops decoding at the Transformers default of 20 steps,
truncating \NCharsiuTrunc{} of \NCharsiuN{} outputs. With 50 steps the WER is \NCharsiuWerFixed, and
\NCharsiuLenOnly{} of \NCharsiuWordErrors{} word errors concern length only. On our text it gets length right on
\NCharsiuSolAll\% of prose and \NCharsiuBeoAll\% of verse tokens (prior: \NWSolAllFreq\% and \NWBeoAllFreq\%), and the
context-free prior repairs \NPCharsiuFixPriorSolShare\% of its \NPCharsiuLexWrongSol{} wrong-length ambiguous prose
tokens.

\paragraph{Limitations.} One test text per genre lets single words decide results (\emph{Gēata}, \emph{þe}), and
training is \NPTrainBrightShare\% Bright's, like the development text. The gold follows one editor, and we have not
checked any transcription against the printed page. An LLM made the labels and one of us, not blind to the systems,
checked them, so there is no human agreement. Three runs per arm cannot rank context arms, ByT5 never ran with
dictionary candidates, and local2 became the headline arm after the test results. Within these caveats, coverage
limits verse most, and convention moves the class~B score more than context does.

\section{Future work}
Training and testing within one editorial tradition, and scoring against several editions at once, would separate
convention from error. Proofread test texts, ByT5 runs with the dictionary candidates, a dictionary-free generator
\cite{gershuni2022} for patterns that no source lists, deterministic training with five or more seeds per arm
\cite{summers2021}, a second human annotator and more verse would remove the main caveats.
