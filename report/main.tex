\begin{abstract}
Editors of Old English add macrons for long vowels that the manuscripts do not write. We ask what limits automatic
restoration of these marks and measure four limits: disagreement between editors, editorial convention, sentence
context and dictionary coverage. Coverage moves the most on verse: a frequency prior on Tauber's \textit{Beowulf}
rises from \NTBeoAllFreq\% to \NABeoAllFreq\% of tokens when dictionaries supply the candidates. Context helps where
grammar decides the reading. On grammatical homographs in Alfred's \textit{Soliloquies} (a class assigned by LLM-based
annotators and not yet reviewed by us), a ByT5 scorer that sees two words on either side reaches
\NEncRepSolClsBLocalTwo\%, against \NEncCapfullSolClsBWord\% for the word alone. It gains nothing on lexical or
editorial homographs, and on all ambiguous \textit{Beowulf} tokens the word alone does as well as any context arm.
Wyatt's and Klaeber's editions of \textit{Beowulf} agree on \NPairWTLex\% of ambiguous tokens and on only
\NPairWTClsB\% of grammatical ones, so part of what remains is editorial convention that no model can read from the
text. CharsiuG2P's published Old English word error rate of \NCharsiuWer{} partly reflects a 20-step decoding limit
(\NCharsiuWerFixed{} without it), and \NCharsiuLenShare\% of its remaining word errors concern vowel length only.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Old English manuscripts do not mark vowel length. Modern editions add macrons, and the marks separate words that are
otherwise spelled alike: \emph{god} `God' and \emph{gōd} `good', the relative particle \emph{þe} and the pronoun
\emph{þē} `thee'. Metrical analysis and grapheme-to-phoneme (G2P) conversion both depend on these lengths. Restoring
them means choosing among candidate length patterns for each bare word.

We ask what limits this restoration and measure four limits: how far two editors agree (the ceiling), whether
training and test editions mark a word alike (editorial convention), whether neighbouring words decide among
candidates (context), and whether the right candidate exists at all (coverage). We also contribute a cleaned,
length-marked corpus of \NCorpusTokens{} tokens from nine editions % data/manifest.json
with work-level splits and a leakage audit.

\paragraph{Related work.} Yarowsky \cite{yarowsky1994} restored Spanish and French accents with decision lists over
local context. For Latin, Winge \cite{winge2015} combines a tagger, a lemmatiser and a morphological analyser and
agrees with a human annotator on about 98\% of vowel lengths. Thörn Cleland and Cullhed \cite{cleland2026} propagate
Ancient Greek length marks across the forms of a lexeme and train a small character transformer on the result.
Esmail et al.\ \cite{esmail2022} measure how much lookahead matters for Arabic diacritisation, the question our
left-context arm asks for Old English. CharsiuG2P \cite{zhu2022} covers Old English in a 100-language ByT5 G2P model
whose Old English data come from WikiPron \cite{lee2020}, but it reads isolated words. Tauber \cite{tauber2021} added
the vowel lengths of the Perseus text of Klaeber's edition \cite{klaeber1922} to Brunetti's token-level tagging of
\textit{Beowulf}, which gives us a gold standard. We know of no evaluation on Old English running text that sets a
model against the disagreement between editors.

\section{Candidates, populations and ambiguity classes}
A word's pattern has one 0/1 slot per vowel letter, and a candidate source proposes the possible patterns for each
bare word. Outside the coverage comparison, all systems take their candidates from the training forms plus WikiPron \cite{lee2020}, whose
Old English IPA we convert to spellings by aligning vowels (\emph{ea, eo, ie, io} count as one) with IPA nuclei
(\NWikiForms{} forms from \NWikiRows{} rows, \NWikiAligned\% aligned). The proposal named three dictionaries: Clark
Hall in TEI \cite{clarkhall}, Wiktionary inflection tables via kaikki.org \cite{ylonen2022} and Bosworth-Toller
\cite{bosworth}. They enter only the coverage comparison (Section~\ref{sec:coverage}), where we rerun the prior and the bilateral model with them. The
frequency prior picks a word's most frequent training pattern and, for an unseen word, the pattern most sources
attest, then the one with fewest long vowels.

Besides all tokens we score two populations. A token is \emph{lexicon-ambiguous} when its word has at least two
candidates (\NWSolLexN{} of \NWSolAllN{} prose and \NWBeoLexN{} of \NWBeoAllN{} verse tokens), and a \emph{minority
reading} when its gold pattern occurs in training but is not the majority. The 60 % data/ambiguity_classes.json, n_skeletons
most frequent ambiguous skeletons also carry a class: \NClassA{} \emph{lexical} (A, two words: \emph{god/gōd}),
\NClassB{} \emph{grammatical} (B, closed-class words of different function: \emph{þe/þē}) and \NClassC{}
\emph{editorial} (C, one word marked by habit: \emph{he/hē}). Two independent LLM-based annotators assigned the
classes (Cohen's $\kappa=\NClassKappa$ \cite{cohen1960}), an automated adjudicator settled their two disagreements,
% data/ambiguity_classes.json, n_disagreements
and we have not yet reviewed the labels.

\section{Systems and protocol}\label{sec:systems}
All systems choose among the same candidates and predict all-short for a word without any. Besides the prior,
logistic classifiers (scikit-learn \cite{pedregosa2011}, log loss) see the word with its first and last two letters
(\emph{word}), plus the two preceding words (\emph{left}), plus the two following words (\emph{bilateral}). Our main
model fine-tunes ByT5-small \cite{xue2022} with Transformers \cite{wolf2020} as a candidate scorer. The input is the
word alone or its context with the target marked \texttt{[[target]]}, the output is the pattern string (e.g.\
\texttt{01}), and at test time each candidate is scored by the summed log-probability of its string. It trains on the
\NEncTrainExamples{} lexicon-ambiguous training tokens. Arms differ only in the input: \emph{word}, \emph{left} (all
preceding words), \emph{sentence} (both sides), \emph{local2} and \emph{local5} (two or five words on each side), cut
at 128, 256 % results/encoder/summary.json, settings.max_bytes
or \NEncFullCap{} bytes (full). Local inputs never exceed 103 bytes, % results/encoder/summary.json, local_replicates.note
so their runs at the three caps share inputs and we pool them.

We chose the training setting on the development set only. Of \NSelRuns{} sentence-arm runs crossing learning rate,
epochs, Zupitza in or out, and two seeds, learning rate $3\cdot10^{-4}$, % results/encoder/selection.json, choice
\NSelEpochs{} epochs and no Zupitza gave the best mean ambiguous-token accuracy. On the \NDevLexShared{} ambiguous development tokens that both settings share,
dropping Zupitza raises ByT5 from \NSelDevZupShared\% (its best setting with Zupitza) to \NSelDevNoZup\%, the prior
from \NDevFreqZupShared\% to \NDevFreqNoZup\% and the bilateral model from \NDevBilZupShared\% to \NDevBilNoZup\%.
The price is coverage: the prior loses \NDThreeSolAllCost{} points on all prose
test tokens. Three seeds per arm and cap, with the word arm run at the full cap only, make \NGridRuns{} runs.

We report means and standard deviations over seeds, and differences with a 95\% interval from a paired bootstrap
\cite{efron1993} that resamples whole sentences 1,000 times, % src/oevl/stats.py
using each token's correctness averaged over seeds. We do not rely on single-seed McNemar tests \cite{mcnemar1947},
because they ignore training variance.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Editors: ceiling and convention}\label{sec:editors}
Table~\ref{tab:editors} compares the three \textit{Beowulf} editions on the tokens each pair aligns. Harrison \& Sharp
and Wyatt agree on \NPairHWLex\% of lexicon-ambiguous tokens, and Wyatt and Tauber equally often to two decimals.
Editors diverge on class B: Wyatt agrees with the other two on only \NPairHWClsB\% and \NPairWTClsB\% of grammatical
homographs, mostly over \emph{ne/nē} and \emph{þe/þē}. % results/agreement/disagreements.tsv
Harrison \& Sharp and Tauber agree far more closely, and the source likely explains part of it. Our Harrison \& Sharp
file is the Project Gutenberg revision of their fourth edition, whose producer changed a listed set of quantities to
modern values, among them \emph{ne} to \emph{nē} as a conjunction \cite{editions}. Of the \NPairHWClsBDis{} class B
disagreements between Harrison \& Sharp and Wyatt, \NPairHWClsBDisNe{} are this \emph{ne/nē}. Neither pair with
Harrison \& Sharp is two independent editors, so we take Wyatt against Tauber's Klaeber text as the two-editor
reference. It gives nearly the same figures.

Convention shows when the gold edition changes. On the \NPairWTClsBN{} class B tokens that Wyatt and Tauber share, the
prior agrees with Wyatt on \NPairWTClsBPriorWyatt\% and with Tauber on \NPairWTClsBPriorTauber\%. Swapping the gold
edition moves its class B accuracy by \NPairWTClsBPriorGap{} points, about twice what context adds for ByT5 on verse
class B (Section~\ref{sec:context}), and against Wyatt the prior agrees more often than the two editors do. Our
training editions mark these words more like Wyatt than like Klaeber.

An LLM-based assistant labelled a random sample of \NCheckN{} Harrison \& Sharp and Wyatt disagreements, and a
second, blind LLM review corrected four of them (not yet checked by us): \NCheckEditorial{} editorial, \NCheckNorm{} macrons on the other letter of a digraph that our
normalisation could align, \NCheckAlign{} alignment faults and \NCheckOther{} others, mostly probable slips.

The proposal promised to report disagreement instead of a model if agreement was low. We fixed what counts as low
only after measuring (agreement at least 10 points above the prior on ambiguous tokens), % results/agreement/metrics.json, gate.rule
so the rule decided nothing. Only Harrison \& Sharp against Tauber clears it, against the Harrison \& Sharp floor only (\NGateHTHs{} points, \NGateHTTauber{} against Tauber's), the pair the
revision likely brought closer. Against Wyatt or Tauber the prior is only \NGateWTWyatt{} and \NGateWTTauber{} points
below the agreement of these two editors.

\subsection{CharsiuG2P}
We reproduce CharsiuG2P's published Old English scores exactly (WER \NCharsiuWer, PER \NCharsiuPer). Its evaluation % results/charsiu/benchmark.json, max_length_20 and max_length_50
stops decoding after 20 steps and so cuts \NCharsiuTrunc{} of its \NCharsiuN{} outputs short. With 50 steps the WER is
\NCharsiuWerFixed, and \NCharsiuLenOnly{} of the \NCharsiuWordErrors{} word errors (\NCharsiuLenShare\%) differ from
the reference only in vowel length, as the proposal expected. On our test tokens, with its \texttt{<ang>:} prefix and its IPA mapped to length patterns, it
gets length right on \NCharsiuSolAll\% of prose and \NCharsiuBeoAll\% of verse tokens, and on \NCharsiuSolLex\% and
\NCharsiuBeoLex\% of the ambiguous ones (our prior: \NWSolAllFreq\%, \NWBeoAllFreq\%, \NWSolLexFreq\%,
\NWBeoLexFreq\%). Our word-only logistic model (seed~1) gets \NCharsiuSolLexWordFix{} of CharsiuG2P's \NCharsiuSolLexWrong{} length
errors on ambiguous prose tokens right (\NCharsiuSolLexWordFixShare\%) and the bilateral model \NCharsiuSolLexBilFix,
so the edition's habit for the word, not context, repairs most of them. CharsiuG2P was built for
word lists from Wiktionary transcriptions, not to follow an edition's conventions in running text, so the gap reflects the
task as much as the model.

\section{Error analysis}
Two independent LLM-based annotators typed \NErrItems{} random lexicon-ambiguous errors from each of the ByT5 sentence
arm (full cap, seed~1) and CharsiuG2P, pooled over both test sets ($\kappa=\NErrKappa$). An automated adjudicator
resolved their disagreements, a second, blind LLM review corrected five labels, and we have not yet reviewed them
(Table~\ref{tab:errors}). The CharsiuG2P sample
was drawn before Zupitza left training. ByT5 errors spread evenly over homographs (\NErrEncHomograph), grammatical
pairs (\NErrEncGrammatical) and editorial habit (\NErrEncEditorial). For \NErrEncGoldInDoubt{} more, another edition
supports the prediction, and for \NErrEncCeiling{} the gold is not a candidate. CharsiuG2P, which sees no context, has
more grammatical (\NErrCharsiuGrammatical) and editorial (\NErrCharsiuEditorial) errors and fewer homograph errors
(\NErrCharsiuHomograph).

Two examples show where context decides and where the gold itself is in doubt. In the \textit{Soliloquies} sentence
\emph{Ic clypie tō þē, Drihten, þū þe æall geworhtest} `I call to thee, Lord, thou who madest all', the first
\emph{þē} is the pronoun and the second \emph{þe} the relative particle. The prior, the logistic models and every
word-arm run mark both short, and all \NToTheEncLocalTwoRuns{} local2 runs get both right. Of the \NToTheN{} long
\emph{þē} after \emph{tō} in this text the word arm finds \NToTheEncWordMax, the local2 runs \NToTheEncLocalTwoMin{} to
\NToTheEncLocalTwoMax, and the sentence-arm runs anywhere from \NToTheEncBilMin{} to \NToTheEncBilMax, the instability
of the previous section in one word. In \textit{Beowulf} line 2796, % data/brunetti-length.txt
\emph{ēcum dryhtne þē ic hēr on starie} `(thanks to) the eternal Lord (for the treasures) that I gaze on here',
\emph{þē} is the relative `which', not `thee'. Tauber and Harrison \& Sharp print \emph{þē}, Wyatt prints \emph{þe},
and every full-cap ByT5 run chooses \emph{þe}. The automated annotators marked this error `gold in doubt': the model
agrees with one editor and is scored against another.

\section{Deviations, limitations and conclusion}
\paragraph{Deviations.} Coverage became one of four limits, so the abstract also reports all-token
accuracy. Instead of an encoder, a fine-tuned encoder-decoder (ByT5) scores the candidates, one
checkpoint per arm on identical data. The trained systems keep the candidate set of our first pipeline, so the proposal's three dictionaries enter only the coverage comparison. LLM-based annotators, not two of us, labelled the ambiguity classes, the error types and the
disagreement sample, and a second, blind LLM review checked all 560 labels.

\paragraph{Limitations.} We predict editorial convention, not pronunciation: the dictionaries and the gold descend
from the same nineteenth-century philology. Tauber's gold is Klaeber's editorial opinion, taken over from the Perseus
text. The 60 classified skeletons were ranked on a corpus that still included Zupitza and
counted the test tokens, though not their gold. % data/ambiguity_classes.json, counting_rule

\paragraph{Conclusion.} Coverage is the largest limit on verse: over the same WikiPron prior, the proposal
dictionaries add \NBeoGainDictWiki{} points on \textit{Beowulf} and the best context arm \NEncBestCtxGainBeo. On prose
the two are closer (\NSolGainDictWiki{} against \NEncBestCtxGainSol). Context helps on grammatical homographs such as
\emph{þe/þē} (\NEncCapfullSolClsBWord\% to \NEncRepSolClsBLocalTwo\% on prose), and two words on each side suffice,
but it leaves lexical and editorial ambiguity untouched. The LLM-based annotators label \NErrEncEditorial{} of
\NErrItems{} sampled ByT5 errors as editorial habit, and on grammatical words two independent editors agree on only
\NPairWTClsB\% of tokens, so a single gold edition cannot tell a wrong model from a different convention. Next we
will train and test within one editorial tradition and check the automated labels ourselves.
