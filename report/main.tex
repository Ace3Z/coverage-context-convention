\begin{abstract}
Many editions of Old English mark long vowels with macrons, but the manuscripts do not mark length systematically, so
bare text merges \emph{god} `God' and \emph{gōd} `good'. We restore the marks by choosing, for each bare word, among
the length patterns that candidate sources list, and we ask what limits the result: missing candidates, too little
context, or the editor's convention. We build a length-marked corpus of nine editions (\NCorpusTokens{} tokens), to our
knowledge the only one besides Tauber's \textit{Beowulf}, and release it with Tauber's text. Missing
candidates are the largest limit: with WikiPron candidates, \NPOutOfReachWSolShare\% of a frequency prior's prose errors
and \NPOutOfReachWBeoShare\% of its verse errors have no correct candidate. Context helps a group of five closed-class
words, chiefly \emph{þe/þē}, and barely changes the rest: on prose, a ByT5 scorer that sees two words on each side
raises accuracy on the five from \NEncCapfullSolClsBWord\% to \NEncCapfullSolClsBLocalTwo\%, and a post hoc logistic
model with word-specific collocations does about as well. On ambiguous verse tokens context adds nothing, and a post
hoc analysis traces this to one unseen name. Convention can outweigh context: swapping the gold between two
\textit{Beowulf} editions moves the prior's score on the five words by \NPairWTClsBPriorGap{} points, \NPSwapRatio{}
times the context gain on the same tokens with Klaeber as gold. CharsiuG2P's published Old English word error rate
(\NCharsiuWer) partly reflects a 20-step decoding limit (\NCharsiuWerFixed{} with 50 steps).

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Many editions of Old English mark long vowels with a macron. The manuscripts do not mark length systematically,
though some Late Old English scribes marked it with accents \cite{mokrowiecki2015}. Bare text merges
\emph{god} `God' and \emph{gōd} `good', or the relative particle \emph{þe} and the pronoun \emph{þē} `thee', so restored
marks could help readers of texts transcribed without them. Restoring
them means choosing, for each word, among the length patterns that candidate sources list: lexical
disambiguation as in accent restoration \cite{yarowsky1994} and homograph disambiguation in text-to-speech
\cite{gorman2018}, here for a historical language with little data. The
ambiguity is lexical (class~A below, \emph{god}), grammatical (class~B, \emph{þe}) or editorial (class~C). We predict an
editor's marking convention, not pronunciation.

To our knowledge, no system has restored Old English vowel length in context. We measure three limits on the same
two test texts. \emph{Coverage} (Section~\ref{sec:coverage}) asks whether the right candidate exists, and with WikiPron
candidates, for most errors of a frequency prior (which picks each word's most frequent pattern), it does not. \emph{Context}
(Section~\ref{sec:context}) asks whether neighbouring words can pick it out, and they help mainly class~B, chiefly
\emph{þe/þē}. \emph{Convention} (Section~\ref{sec:convention}) asks whose marking the gold follows, and on class~B the
choice of \textit{Beowulf} edition moves the prior more than context adds. We contribute a length-marked corpus of nine
editions (Section~\ref{sec:data}, to our knowledge the only one besides Tauber's \textit{Beowulf} \cite{tauber2021}),
an evaluation that separates the three limits, a reanalysis of CharsiuG2P \cite{zhu2022} (Section~\ref{sec:errors})
and tested code whose one script recomputes every result here from the released data and model predictions (MIT licence).

\paragraph{Related work.} Yarowsky \cite{yarowsky1994} restored Spanish and French accents with decision lists over
word-specific collocations, taking accented text as a fully objective standard, which Old English editions are not.
Latin \cite{winge2015} and Ancient Greek \cite{cleland2026} macronisers use taggers, lemmatisers and morphological
analysers or large pretraining \cite{cullhed2026}. Winge \cite{winge2015} already bounded his Latin system with a lexicon oracle and
traced the largest group of its disagreements with a hand-marked text to marking conventions. Gershuni and Pinter
\cite{gershuni2022} restore Hebrew diacritics with a character tagger instead of candidates, and Esmail et al.\
\cite{esmail2022} measure how much lookahead helps Arabic diacritisation. We test lookahead with a model that sees only
preceding words. Plank \cite{plank2022} argues that one gold label hides annotator
variation, which here includes editorial convention.

\section{Data set}\label{sec:data}
Table~\ref{tab:data} lists the corpus: nine editions printed from 1880 to 1921 with \NCorpusTokens{} tokens in
\NPCorpusSentences{} sentences, plus Tauber's \textit{Beowulf}. We took Project Gutenberg and Wikisource
transcriptions \cite{zupitza1880,bright1913,bright1910,sweet1905,cook1921,bright1904,hargrove1902,harrison1883,wyatt1894} and cleaned them with character mappings, each counted in a manifest (some files cannot
be rebuilt exactly from this repository). The Hugging Face release (CC BY-SA
4.0) keeps each sentence's work, editor, year, genre and section, per-token length patterns and a leakage flag.
We built the corpus because word lists lack context, treebanks such as YCOE \cite{ycoe2003} carry, as far as we know,
no length marks, and Tauber's \textit{Beowulf} is a single poem. Our corpus has \NPCorpusOverTauber{} times its
tokens, and together they hold three editions of \textit{Beowulf} to compare.

\section{Preprocessing}\label{sec:prep}
Preprocessing normalises Unicode (NFC), keeps the macron as the only diacritic, drops English and Latin lines by their
function words, splits sentences at end punctuation and takes letter runs as tokens. A token's \emph{key} is its lower-cased form without macrons, and its
\emph{pattern} has one digit per vowel letter, 1 for a macron: Harrison and Sharp's \emph{geārdagum} (0100) and
Klaeber's \emph{gēardagum} (1000) share the key \emph{geardagum} and differ only in which digraph letter bears the macron.

A key's candidates are its training patterns plus those of every listed form with that key. Our main list is WikiPron
\cite{lee2020}, whose IPA we convert to spellings by aligning vowel units with IPA nuclei (\NWikiAligned\% of
\NWikiRows{} rows align). A coverage comparison adds the dictionaries of our proposal, Clark Hall \cite{clarkhall},
Wiktionary via kaikki.org \cite{ylonen2022} and Bosworth-Toller \cite{bosworth}, though WikiPron is itself mined from
Wiktionary (\NPWikiInWiktionaryShare\% of its forms are in the Wiktionary list). A token is \emph{lexicon-ambiguous}
when its key has two or more candidates, as for \NPLexShareSol\% of prose and \NPLexShareBeo\% of verse tokens with
WikiPron. As adding sources enlarges this set, we compare sources on all tokens. The \NPClassLabels{} most frequent
ambiguous keys (ranked before we dropped Zupitza, with test tokens counted but not their gold) carry a class: \NClassA{} lexical (A:
\emph{god/gōd}), \NClassB{} grammatical (B: \emph{þe, ne, ge, gyt, þis}) and \NClassC{} editorial (C: \emph{se/sē,
he/hē}), covering \NPClsShareSol\% of prose and \NPClsShareBeo\% of verse ambiguous tokens.

\paragraph{Labels.} The classes, the types of \NPErrLabels{} sampled errors and a sample of \NCheckN{} editor
disagreements were labelled by a large language model, Claude Opus 5.5 \cite{claude2026}, run as separate annotator
agents with written guidelines. For the classes and the error types, two runs of the model labelled every item
separately (Cohen's $\kappa$ \NClassKappa{} and \NErrKappa{} \cite{cohen1960}), and a third run settled their
disagreements. A single run labelled the disagreement sample. A blind review by further runs of the same model changed
\NPReviewChanged{} of the \NPReviewItems{} labels. The labels were then manually checked, and none was changed. As both
annotators are runs of one model, $\kappa$ measures that model's consistency rather than agreement between people, and
as the manual check was not blind to the model's labels, we lack the comparison with independent human labels that
Pangakis et al.\ \cite{pangakis2023} call for.

\section{Model training}\label{sec:training}
\paragraph{Baselines.} All our systems are supervised, choose among the same candidates and leave a word without
candidates unmarked. The frequency prior, fixed in our proposal after Yarowsky \cite{yarowsky1994}, picks a word's most
frequent training pattern and, for an unseen word, the pattern most sources list, with ties going to fewer long
vowels. Logistic classifiers (scikit-learn \cite{pedregosa2011}, untuned, three seeds) see sparse indicators of the
word and its first and last two letters (\emph{word}), plus the two preceding words (\emph{left}), plus the two
following words (\emph{bilateral}). These context features are not conjoined with the word, so `previous word
\emph{tō}' shifts every word's scores alike. After the ByT5 results we added a post hoc \emph{conjoined} variant with the
word-by-neighbour features of earlier work \cite{yarowsky1994,gorman2018}.

\paragraph{ByT5.} As our main model we fine-tune ByT5-small \cite{xue2022} with PyTorch \cite{paszke2019} and
Transformers \cite{wolf2020} to score candidates. ByT5 reads bytes, so thorn, eth and ash
need no vocabulary. It is also CharsiuG2P's backbone, and scoring candidates guarantees a valid output.
The input is the bare word, alone or in context with the target marked
\texttt{[[ ]]}, the output is its pattern string, and a candidate's score is the summed log-probability of its bytes,
free of length bias since candidates are equally long. Arms differ only in the input: \emph{word}, \emph{left} (all
preceding words), \emph{sentence}, \emph{local2} and \emph{local5} (two or five words on each side), all trained on the
same \NEncTrainExamples{} ambiguous training tokens as the logistic models. We report the
full input cap (\NEncFullCap{} bytes), as 128- and 256-byte caps move mean ambiguous-token accuracy by under two
points.

\paragraph{Protocol.} We chose the setting (learning rate $3\cdot10^{-4}$, \NSelEpochs{} epochs, no Zupitza) from
\NSelRuns{} sentence-arm runs on development data and reused it for every arm. Dropping Zupitza's \textit{Grammar},
which marks \emph{þes}, \emph{gyt} and \emph{lytel} short, lifts the prior on ambiguous development tokens from
\NDevFreqZupShared\% to \NDevFreqNoZup\% and the bilateral model from \NDevBilZupShared\% to \NDevBilNoZup\%. ByT5
gains too in all but the least trained of the \NPDThreeEncSettings{} settings run both ways: the change spans
$\NPDThreeEncMin$ to $\NPDThreeEncMax$ points (we leave out a sixth, where one run chose the candidate with most long
vowels for \NCollapseDevMostLong\% of ambiguous development tokens). The price, measured afterwards, is coverage: only Zupitza writes
\emph{forþām}, \emph{swāswā} and \emph{forþī} as one word, so \NPZupWords{} prose test tokens become unknown and the
prior loses \NDThreeSolAllCost{} points. The grid has \NGridRuns{} runs (four context arms at three caps and the word
arm, three seeds each). About fifty exploratory runs were scored on the test sets before the development set existed,
and the selection runs also logged test scores. The choice used development scores only.

\paragraph{Changes from the proposal.} The corpus grew to nine editions. A ByT5 encoder-decoder replaced the planned
encoder classifier. WikiPron superseded the proposed dictionaries as the main candidate source. We added the logistic
baselines and the local2 and local5 arms, and chose local2 as the headline arm after the test results. The class~B focus and the gold swap
of Section~\ref{sec:convention} were not planned. An LLM labelled what we had planned to label ourselves. We set the
threshold for reporting editor disagreement instead of a model only after measuring, so it decided nothing.

\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}
Our main finding: missing candidates cost more than context adds on both test texts, and on the \textit{Beowulf} class~B tokens
the choice of gold edition moves the prior's score more than context adds. We score token accuracy, as a reader needs
the whole word: a token counts only if every vowel has the gold length. On all tokens, marking nothing
scores \NPAllShortSol\% (prose) and \NPAllShortBeo\% (verse), the WikiPron prior \NWSolAllFreq\% and \NWBeoAllFreq\%, and local2
\NEncCapfullSolAllLocalTwo\% and \NEncCapfullBeoAllLocalTwo\%. Differences carry 95\% intervals from a paired bootstrap
over sentences \cite{efron1993}, whose tokens are not independent (1,000 draws, correctness averaged over runs,
runs held fixed). Intervals and $p$-values are not corrected for multiple comparisons, and we claim no difference
between ByT5 arms below about one point.

\subsection{Coverage}\label{sec:coverage}
Most of the prior's errors are beyond any chooser: with WikiPron, \NPOutOfReachWSolShare\% of its prose errors and
\NPOutOfReachWBeoShare\% of its verse errors have no correct candidate, mostly because the word is unknown. Our
proposal had assumed that every error is a selection error. Both tests have about the same achievable ceiling, a
perfect chooser's score (Figure~\ref{fig:coverage}, \NWSolAllOrc\% and \NWBeoAllOrc\%), and a better chooser could gain
at most \NPHeadroomSolPts{} points over the prior on prose and \NPHeadroomBeoPts{} on verse. Dictionaries change the prior only for words
unseen in training, as a dictionary-only pattern has no training count: on unseen verse tokens the prior rises from
\NPUnseenPriorTBeo\% to \NPUnseenPriorABeo\%. Of the verse gain from all four
sources (\NBeoGainDict{} points), \NPBeoOnlyGainShare\% falls on \textit{Beowulf}-only word types, so it may
not hold for other verse.

\subsection{Context}\label{sec:context}
Context helps class~B and barely moves classes A and C. On prose, local2 (chosen after the test results) beats the word arm by
\dci{NEncCapfullSolLexLocalTwoVsWord} points on ambiguous tokens (Table~\ref{tab:context}), almost all in class~B.
There it lifts accuracy from \NEncCapfullSolClsBWord\%, where the word arm equals the prior, to
\NEncCapfullSolClsBLocalTwo\% (\dci{NEncCapfullSolClsBLocalTwoVsWord}, or
\iv{\NPTwoLevelSolClsBLocalTwoVsWordLo}{\NPTwoLevelSolClsBLocalTwoVsWordHi} with runs resampled). Every context arm
shows this gain, even the left arm, which sees no following word. Most of it comes from telling
relative \emph{þe} from the pronoun \emph{þē} `thee' (\NPPwSolThePrior\% to \NPPwSolTheLocalTwo\% on
\NPPwSolTheN{} tokens). In \emph{Ic clypie tō þē, Drihten, þū þe æall geworhtest} `I call to thee, Lord, thou who
madest all', the prior, the ByT5 word runs and the logistic word, left and bilateral runs mark both \emph{þe} short,
while every local2 run and every conjoined run gets the pronoun long and the relative short.

On verse, one name masks the context gain: local2 gains \dci{NEncCapfullBeoClsBLocalTwoVsWord} points on class~B,
mostly from \emph{þe}, yet on ambiguous verse tokens no context arm clearly beats the word arm (local2
\dci{NEncCapfullBeoLexLocalTwoVsWord}). \emph{Gēata} `of the Geats' (\NPGeataN{} tokens, all long) is unseen in training, and WikiPron
also lists \emph{geata} `gates'. The prior falls back to fewer long vowels and misses every token, the word arm gets
all in every seed, and the sentence arm almost none. Without \emph{Gēata} (post hoc), local2 minus word
is \dci{NPBeoLexNoGeataLocalTwoVsWord} and sentence minus word \dci{NPBeoLexNoGeataBilVsWord}, both below our
one-point margin. On all prose tokens local2 adds \NPEncGainLocalTwoSol{} points to the WikiPron prior
(sentence arm \NPEncGainBilSol) out of the \NPHeadroomSolPts{} a better chooser could add. The three other
dictionaries add more (\NSolGainDictWiki). On all verse tokens the word arm alone does as well as local2
(\NEncCapfullBeoAllWord\% and \NEncCapfullBeoAllLocalTwo\%).

The bilateral logistic model gains less over its word model (prose class~B \dci{NBootSolClsBBilVsWord}), as its
features cannot express that `previous word \emph{tō}' matters for \emph{þe} alone. The post hoc conjoined variant can,
and on prose we cannot tell it from local2 (local2 minus conjoined on class~B: \dci{NPLocalTwoVsConjSolClsB}), while
on ambiguous verse tokens local2 leads by about a point (\dci{NPLocalTwoVsConjBeoLex}).
Training variance limits the table: over the sentence arm's nine runs at the three caps, prose class~B spans
\NEncRunsSolClsBBilMin\% to \NEncRunsSolClsBBilMax\%. Local inputs never reach a cap, so local
runs of one seed repeat the same training at each cap, yet local5 replicates differ by up to
\NEncRepSolClsBLocalFiveSpread{} points on prose class~B. Seeds \cite{dodge2020} and tiny nondeterminism
\cite{summers2021} move training results. We set no deterministic mode and did not test the cause, and
three runs per arm cannot rank the context arms.

\subsection{Convention}\label{sec:convention}
On class~B, which edition serves as gold matters more than context. Table~\ref{tab:editors} compares the three
\textit{Beowulf} editions on the tokens each pair aligns (identical
spelling runs, \NPAlignMin\% to \NPAlignMax\% of each text). Our Harrison and Sharp file, a Project Gutenberg
revision that modernised some quantities (conjunctive \emph{ne} to \emph{nē}), sits close to Klaeber, so Wyatt
against Klaeber is our two-editor reference. They agree on \NPairWTLex\% of ambiguous tokens but only
on \NPairWTClsB\% of class~B. Both texts nearly always mark the pronouns \emph{þē} `thee' and \emph{gē} `ye' long, but
in \NPWTClsBDisWyattShort{} of their \NPWTClsBDis{} class~B disagreements Klaeber marks a \emph{nē}, \emph{gȳt}
`yet', \emph{gē} `and' or relative \emph{þē} long and Wyatt's text does not. Of a labelled sample of \NCheckN{}
disagreements between Wyatt's text and Harrison and Sharp's, \NCheckEditorial{} are editorial, \NCheckNorm{} put the macron on the
other letter of a digraph and \NCheckOther{} are other, mostly apparent slips in one text, so the editions probably
agree on length more often than we measure. On the \NPairWTClsBN{} shared class~B tokens, the prior agrees with Wyatt
on \NPairWTClsBPriorWyatt\% and with Klaeber on \NPairWTClsBPriorTauber\%, a swing of \NPairWTClsBPriorGap{} points.
Against Wyatt the prior even beats the editors' own agreement, which is therefore no ceiling on accuracy.
Local2 gains \dci{NPSwapGainTauber} points over the word arm with Klaeber as gold and \dci{NPSwapGainWyatt} with
Wyatt. The prior's swing is thus \NPSwapRatio{} times the larger context gain, and local2's own score moves with the gold too
(\NPSwapLocalTwoWyatt\% against Wyatt, \NPSwapLocalTwoTauber\% against Klaeber).\looseness=-1

The article \emph{se} shows convention most clearly. Klaeber writes it short in \NPSeArticleShort{} of \NPSeArticle{}
article tokens and long in all \NPSeOtherLong{} pronoun and relative tokens (by Tauber's glosses), while
our training editions write \emph{sē} \NPSeTrainLong{} times against \NPSeTrainShort{} \emph{se}. The prior gets \NPSeArticlePrior{} of the short
articles right and no ByT5 run more than \NPSeArticleBestRun, and in line 2587, \emph{þæt se mǣra maga Ecgðēowes}, the
prior and all \NPSeLineLongRuns{} ByT5 runs mark it long. Klaeber's choice follows syntax and could be learned from
Klaeber-style text, but a model cannot tell from bare text which convention the gold follows.

\subsection{Errors, CharsiuG2P and limitations}\label{sec:errors}
ByT5's prose errors are most often grammatical
(\NPErrEncSolGrammatical{} of \NPErrEncSolN, \emph{þe} \NPErrEncSolGramThe{} times) and its verse errors most often
homographs (\NPErrEncBeoHomograph{} of \NPErrEncBeoN, \emph{Gēata} \NPErrEncBeoHomGeata{} times).
Table~\ref{tab:errors} classifies \NErrItems{} random ambiguous errors of the ByT5 sentence arm (seed~1, full cap) and
\NErrItems{} of CharsiuG2P (with Zupitza in the lexicon). The two systems'
profiles over A, B and C differ within verse ($\chi^2$ test in SciPy \cite{virtanen2020}, $p = \NPErrChiBeo$) and
within prose ($p = \NPErrChiSol$), in opposite directions, but not when pooled (\mbox{$p = \NPErrChiAll$}). A fixed rule decides \NPErrRuleDecided{} of the \NPErrLabels{} items, so label
agreement is high partly by construction ($\kappa$ \NPErrKappaNoClass{} on the \NPErrItemsNoClass{} items without a
class). In line 2796, \emph{ēcum dryhtne þē ic hēr on starie}, every full-cap ByT5 run writes Wyatt's \emph{þe} and
is scored wrong against Klaeber's \emph{þē}.

\paragraph{CharsiuG2P.} CharsiuG2P \cite{zhu2022} is a multilingual ByT5 model that reads isolated words, trained on
pronunciation lists (WikiPron's for Old English). It was built for dictionary pronunciation, so our scores
measure how well it fits our editors' marking in running text. We run it
as published (prefix \texttt{<ang>:}) and map its IPA to patterns with our aligner, counting unmappable outputs as
wrong. We reproduce its published Old English scores exactly (WER \NCharsiuWer, PER \NCharsiuPer). Its evaluation
stops decoding at Transformers' default of 20 steps, truncating \NCharsiuTrunc{} of \NCharsiuN{} outputs. With 50
steps the WER is \NCharsiuWerFixed, and \NCharsiuLenOnly{} of \NCharsiuWordErrors{} word errors are length-only.
On our text it gets length right on \NCharsiuSolAll\% of prose and \NCharsiuBeoAll\% of verse tokens, well below the
prior, and of CharsiuG2P's \NPCharsiuLexWrongSol{} ambiguous prose length errors the context-free prior repairs
\NPCharsiuFixPriorSolShare\% from word habit alone.

\paragraph{Limitations.} One test text per genre lets single words decide results (\emph{Gēata}, \emph{þe}), and
\NPTrainBrightShare\% of training is Bright's, as is the development text, so choices made on it may favour his
conventions. The gold follows one editor, and we have not proofread the transcriptions against the print. An LLM made
the labels, and the manual check was not blind and had no second annotator, so no agreement between human annotators
was measured.

\section{Future work}
If convention explains most \textit{Beowulf} class~B and \emph{se} errors, training and testing in one editorial
tradition should reduce them, and scoring against several editions would show what remains. The main caveats
call for proofread test texts, ByT5 with all four sources, a dictionary-free tagger \cite{gershuni2022} for
unlisted patterns (weaker than dictionary-based systems on unseen words), deterministic training with five or more seeds
\cite{summers2021}, more verse and independent human labels for a sample.
